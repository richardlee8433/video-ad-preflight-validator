Video ad pre-flight · v3 findings

What real ads showed that test ads could not

v2 scored 14 out of 14 on synthetic test ads with known ground truth. Two real broadcast ads exposed problems the synthetic ads were too clean to show. Each finding below has v2's own screen as evidence, the video frame showing what was really there, and the v3 result for the same file.

1Rules that cannot apply failed the ad

v2 applied every rule to every ad. A charity spot and a health PSA were each failed for having no alcohol responsibility line and no logo of Northlight, the fictional beer brand the demo rules were written for. A missing disclaimer was then failed again by every rule about its timing and placement. v3: each rule says which ads it covers (applies_when: category, brand), the ad's brand and category are declared, and a rule with nothing to measure is N/A. Only the "present" rule judges whether something is missing.

2Captions, an opening logo card and an end-card logo were missed

v2's prompt had no type for text the editor puts on screen, and its frame trimming kept only the biggest picture changes. A caption that slides in while the camera holds still is a small change, so it could be dropped before the model ever saw it. v3: Apple Vision OCR reads every changed frame (about four a second); frame selection keeps full coverage plus the frames where new text appears; the model gets each frame's OCR lines and a super type. OCR also times each text item from every changed frame. Checked against OCR at 8 frames a second, that timing was within 0.15 s.

What v3 then found: 11 of the PSA's 13 subtitles are on screen for less time than BCAP's reading-time guidance for supers (0.2 s a word plus 2–3 s). They follow the speech, as subtitles do. Whether that guidance should apply to subtitles of the voice-over is a reviewer's call; v3 shows the evidence instead of hiding the text.

3Detected text was hidden, so the page looked empty

v2 did detect the PSA's subtitles, as ten "other text" items, but its timeline drew only slates, disclaimers, logos and claims. The page showed an empty "Model detected" lane for an ad with text on screen for its whole length. v3: every detection type is drawn, and supers have their own colour.

4One logo came back as seven pieces

The Navy-Marine Corps logo is on the backdrop behind the speakers for most of the ad. v2 returned it as seven intervals, several overlapping or touching end to end. v3: intervals of the same item (same type and text) that overlap or are less than a second apart are joined, and the timeline says how many were joined. The gaps that remain are real: the ad cuts to archive footage and an office.

5Text positions were the search window, not the text

v2 measured a text box from the pixels that change when the text appears. When text arrives on a scene cut, every pixel changes, and v2 returned its whole search window as the box. Every subtitle in the PSA got the same box, from 65% of the frame height to the bottom edge, so the title-safe check was measuring the window. v3: boxes come from OCR, read in the middle of the item's time on screen because text often fades in. A box only the model estimated (a logo, or a system without OCR) cannot pass or fail a 5% margin: it is REVIEW.

Nothing broke on the test ads

On the six synthetic test ads with ground truth, v3 finds all 14 objects, as v2 did, and its end-time error is lower (0.21 s against 0.64 s). With the test ads declared as Northlight beer ads, every destination and layer verdict is the same as v2's: 36 of 36.

Not solved yet

  • Run-to-run variation. The model does not return exactly the same items for the same file every time. OCR makes text detection far steadier, but logos, which OCR cannot always read, still vary.
  • Text size over footage. BCAP measures x-height. That is reliable on a flat caption block and unreliable on text laid straight over moving pictures, so a small reading there is REVIEW, not FAIL.
  • OCR needs macOS (Apple Vision). On another system boxes fall back to the model's estimate, which v3 reports as REVIEW for position checks.

Re-checked after recording

Reading the recorded runs against the video frames turned up four more verdicts the evidence could not carry. Each is now a pass on evidence or a person's check:

  • Duration. Both ads failed Amazon's 60 s and 30 s slots by 0.027 s and 0.072 s. The rule now allows half a second.
  • Two-line name captions. The title line under a name is smaller than the name, but the size check used the larger line and passed. It now uses the smallest line (20 TV lines here), and a super it could not measure makes the check REVIEW.
  • Hold time within one frame. A caption 0.05 s short of its hold time failed, although timing comes from frames 0.25 s apart. Shortfalls under one sampled frame are REVIEW.
  • Figures in the voice-over. The model reported "first-of-its-kind" as a claim but not "$47 million in support to 211,000 Marines and Sailors". Figures in the transcript that no claim covers are now listed for review.
